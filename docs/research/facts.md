## Bite 3 measurements and findings, 2026-10-06, macOS arm64, Node 24.15.0, qmd 2.8.3

- qmd's lexical query orders by `bm25_score` alone (`dist/store.js`: the FTS5 CTE interpolates `LIMIT ${ftsLimit}` and the outer query binds `LIMIT ?`, neither with a second sort key). Rows with equal scores come back in insertion order, and the indexer inserts in `fastGlob` traversal order, which is not sorted. Observed: three fresh indexes of the same 736-page corpus answered one of twenty-five benchmark questions two ways, because a pool of twenty cut inside a group of equal scores. The search policy completes the tie group at every cut; see `docs/research/benchmark-lexical.md`.
- Observed per-term pools: at a pool of twenty, 30 of 214 question-term queries over the corpus ended on a tie (the last two rows of a full pool shared a score), and 119 were full without the gold page.
- The public corpus (757 files, 736 pages) indexes in about 665 ms into a 10.8 MiB (11.3 MB) SQLite file; the fixtures index in 2 to 16 ms into 120 to 190 KiB. Query latency through the policy: under 1 ms when the all-terms rung answers, about 9 ms median when the relaxed rung runs twelve per-term queries.
- The benchmark's four numbers are in `docs/research/benchmark-lexical.md`; the raw per-question lines stay under `bench/results/` (gitignored) with the run's metadata.
- Filters and pool, measured after the build review (`bench/report.mjs` renders the note): the gold page's own folder as topic lifts the question form from 17 to 20 of 25 at the top and never lowers a rank; its type does the same; a relaxed per-term pool of 100 moves three questions up and three down against the first rung's pool of 20, so the default stands.
- Cost of a search, measured after the Opus build review: every `searchLex` row carries its page body. Before the fix, 1 000 pages of 50 KB with a topic matching nothing cost one search 18 queries, 8 421 rows, 422 MB of bodies, 238 MB more resident memory and 423 ms (the review's probe). With the relaxed per-term pool at `limit × 4`, the corpus's question form fetches median 210 and at most 515 rows per search; a relaxed pool of 100 fetches median 937 and at most 2 445. The benchmark process reached 1.1 GB resident after 175 searches, a high-water mark of transient bodies rather than a leak as far as measured. An upstream option to return rows without bodies would remove most of this cost.
- Frequency floor: on the corpus, 16 of 25 benchmark questions carry at least one term the engine scores at its floor (`agent`, `model`, `tool`, `base`, `one`, `call`, `context`, `code`, `first`, `spec`, `ai`, `run`, `use`, `llms`, `concept`, `harness`); skipping them on the relaxed rung cost one hit@1 and one hit@3 on the question form and removed the reorder by the company name.
